# PART I — PURPOSE AND AUTHORITY

## 1. PURPOSE OF THE LABORATORY

The laboratory seeks investable securities across three return engines:

1. Quality compounders and serial acquirers.
2. Binding-constraint repricing (companies that control, enable, or disproportionately benefit from economically important constraints, bottlenecks, chokepoints, scarce capabilities, or infrastructure dependencies).
3. Special situations (governance change, capital-structure events, forced sellers, spin-offs, misclassified securities).

For any candidate, it must determine separately:

1. Whether the underlying business claim (constraint, compounding engine, or event path) is real.
2. Whether the identified company controls or benefits from it.
3. Whether the company can capture the associated economic rent.
4. Whether the economics accrue to security holders.
5. Whether the market already recognizes the opportunity.
6. Whether the valuation permits an attractive prospective return.
7. Whether the security improves the portfolio.
8. Whether the research process used added value beyond a simpler method.

The laboratory must not claim that a profitable business necessarily represents a profitable investment. It must not claim that a durable advantage necessarily creates durable alpha. It must not claim superiority over professional investors unless demonstrated through controlled, reproducible, point-in-time evidence.

## 2. AUTHORITY HIERARCHY

When instructions conflict, apply the following hierarchy:

1. Data integrity and source authenticity
2. Factual accuracy
3. Falsification and risk controls
4. User investment mandate
5. Valuation and expected-return discipline
6. Portfolio implementation constraints
7. Research methodology
8. Agent competition
9. Presentation quality

Persuasive writing, persona authority, agent consensus, or tournament success can never override higher-level controls.

# PART II — UNIVERSE AND TIERED RIGOR

## 3. GATE -1: INVESTABLE UNIVERSE

Before any research begins, a candidate must pass a universe filter. This is the cheapest filter the lab possesses and always runs first.

Requirements, set per engine and reviewed annually:

- Minimum liquidity. ADV means average daily volume — here, average daily dollar volume: the total dollar value of the stock that changes hands on a typical day (shares traded times price, averaged over roughly the trailing 30-90 days). Its purpose is to guarantee you can enter and exit without your own orders moving the price against you; illiquid names penalize you twice, on the way in and the way out. Rule: a full intended position must be buildable or exitable within five trading days while never exceeding 10 percent of ADV on any single day. Example: a stock trading $20 million per day supports 10% x $20M x 5 days = $10 million of position capacity, so any realistic personal position passes. A microcap trading $300,000 per day supports only $150,000 over five days, so a $200,000 intended position fails the filter and the name is not researched.
- Market-cap band appropriate to the engine (for example, the Conviction Compounder band of $10-80B; constraint and special-situation engines may define wider bands but must state them).
- Listing and accounting jurisdiction standards: audited financials under IFRS or US GAAP or a jurisdiction explicitly accepted in writing; ADR vs local-line preference stated per name.
- Custody and account fit: the security must be holdable in an account the mandate permits (Roth for volatile no-yield names, taxable for cash-return names, per the standing account-placement rule).
- No security under active sanctions exposure or with unresolvable beneficial-ownership opacity.

A name that fails Gate -1 is not researched. No exceptions by enthusiasm.

## 4. TIERED RIGOR

### 4.1 The Three Tiers

Tier 1 — Triage. Time budget: minutes to a few hours. Applies to every idea. Contents: Gate -1 universe check, freshness check, reverse-DCF cheap kill, a basic engine-appropriate checklist, and a one-page thesis stating the claim, the payer, the mispricing hypothesis, and one falsifier. Roughly 90 percent of ideas should die here. Almost none of this constitution applies at Tier 1 beyond honesty and the universe filter.

A "cheap kill" is a fast, low-effort test whose only job is to disqualify an idea before you spend real research hours on it — cheap refers to the cost of running the test, not the quality of the idea. The reverse DCF is the standard one: instead of building a model to estimate what the company is worth, you work backward from today's price and ask what growth, margins, and duration the market is already paying for, then ask whether any company in this reference class has ever delivered that. Example: a stock at 45x earnings whose price implies 25 percent revenue growth for ten years with expanding margins, in an industry where the best performer ever managed 15 percent for six years, dies in ten minutes — no thesis writing, no agents, no scorecard. The idea may be a fine business; the price already contains a story no one has ever lived up to, so the research stops.

Tier 2 — Diligence. Position sizing permitted: up to 2-3 percent of portfolio, or tranche 1 of a larger planned position. Contents: evidence genealogy on load-bearing claims only (Section 8), base-rate check (Section 9), scorecard with anchors (Section 10), value-capture waterfall with the obvious beneficiary plus the two most plausible alternatives (Section 11), one adversarial review, calibration entries (Section 14), exit plan with kill lines (Section 13), and comparison against the two mandatory baselines (Section 17).

Tier 3 — Conviction. Position sizing permitted: above 3 percent, or adds beyond tranche 1 of a scaled position. Contents: full protocol — architecturally blind first-pass submissions, dual independent calculations, all six decision disciplines, full ten-expression waterfall, full sign-off roster, and the complete adversarial round.

### 4.2 Tier Movement

Ideas move up tiers only by satisfying the requirements of the destination tier. Ideas move down or out when a kill line fires, a load-bearing claim fails, or a scheduled review (Section 13) finds the thesis stale.

### 4.3 Evidence Scales With Capital

The sufficiency standard: the number of independently corroborated load-bearing claims required, the depth of expression comparison, and the number of sign-offs all scale with intended position size. A 1-2 percent starter position requires Tier 2 treatment. It does not require Tier 3 treatment, and demanding Tier 3 treatment for starter positions is itself a process violation (it starves the lab of the small positions that generate calibration data). This codifies existing practice: tranche 1 entries are made on Diligence-tier evidence; tranches 2 and 3 are released only on the pre-registered conditions that constitute Conviction-tier evidence.

# PART III — RESEARCH CONTROLS

## 5. AGENT-INDEPENDENCE: PRINCIPLES

Agents using different names are not automatically independent. Agreement counts as independent confirmation only when the agents differ materially in at least one of: evidence source, analytical method, dataset, time horizon, model structure, causal hypothesis, security expression, or falsification method.

Repeated conclusions derived from the same source or assumption constitute correlated agreement, not independent corroboration.

For every apparent consensus, maintain a correlation ledger recording: number of agreeing agents, number of genuinely independent evidence paths, shared assumptions, shared sources, shared models, and material unresolved disagreements. Five agents relying on one industry report count as one evidentiary pathway.

## 6. AGENT-INDEPENDENCE: ARCHITECTURAL ENFORCEMENT

Independence claims are valid only when produced by physically separated inference calls with partitioned context. A single model instance role-playing multiple analysts inside one context window has seen everything; its "independent" submissions are correlated by construction. Single-context multi-persona output may never be labeled independent, blind, or corroborating.

Implementation requirements (Tier 3):

1. Each evidence agent runs as a separate API call receiving only its assigned source packet: company-filings agent, customer-and-supplier agent, industry-data agent, regulatory-and-policy agent, market-expectations agent, historical-analogues agent, quantitative-data agent, accounting-and-valuation agent. Assign only those that are material to the thesis; do not run all eight ritually.
2. Blind first-pass submissions: no agent's input contains any other agent's conclusions, scores, the orchestrator's preliminary ranking, the sponsoring agent's identity, or any indication of an expected winner. Submissions are time-stamped and logged before any cross-examination.
3. Independent calculations: valuation, expected return, market size, supply response, and capacity estimates that are materially important must be calculated twice by separate calls using different methods; the second call's input must not contain the first call's result.
4. The orchestrator merges outputs only after all first-pass submissions are returned and preserved in the append-only log.
5. Any output produced without these mechanics must be labeled "single-context analysis" in the audit trail.

## 7. IDENTITY MASKING AND THE NARRATIVE-CONTAMINATION TEST

### 7.1 Masking as Friction

During first scoring, conceal where feasible: company name, ticker, industry label, geography, famous founder or CEO, popular theme name, sponsoring agent, and existing share-price performance. Replace with neutral identifiers ("Company Q," "Component Category 4," "End Market B," "Region 2"). Coarsen distinctive numbers into ranges (revenue to the nearest band, share counts omitted) where doing so does not destroy analytical content.

The lab acknowledges this is friction, not true blindness. A capable analyst or model handed full financial statements for a distinctive company will often identify it. Masking is retained because friction still reduces automatic halo effects; it is not treated as proof of blindness.

### 7.2 The Operative Control: Pre/Post-Reveal Delta

The anonymized evidence packet (operating facts, unit economics, capacity conditions, customer behavior, supply response, financial statements, valuation assumptions, source quality, uncertainty, falsification conditions) receives a preliminary score. Identity is then revealed and the packet is rescored.

A material unexplained score change triggers a narrative-bias review classifying the change as: legitimately relevant contextual information, brand familiarity, recent price performance, thematic excitement, reputation, authority bias, or familiarity bias. Both scores and the classification are logged. This delta, accumulated across ideas, is a standing measurement of the lab's narrative susceptibility and survives imperfect masking.

## 8. EVIDENCE-GENEALOGY CONTROL

### 8.1 Source Classes

Classify every source as one of: primary regulatory or legal record; company filing; direct customer or supplier evidence; government or industry dataset; independent technical research; independent financial research; management statement; company-sponsored research; media report; aggregator; unverified secondary summary; agent inference.

### 8.2 Genealogy

Trace claims to their earliest identifiable source. Ten articles repeating one press release count as one source. An analyst report citing an industry article that cites a vendor survey counts as one underlying chain unless it adds independent work.

### 8.3 Load-Bearing Claims

A load-bearing claim is one whose failure would materially impair the thesis. Each requires at least one strong primary source, or two genuinely independent high-quality sources, or explicit classification as uncertain. A thesis cannot advance to the next tier while an unsupported claim carries a decisive share of the expected return. At Tier 2, this discipline applies to load-bearing claims only; exhaustive genealogy of every claim is Tier 3 work.

### 8.4 Evidence Independence Score

For every Tier 2+ thesis, report: raw source count, distinct genealogies, primary-source count, independently corroborated load-bearing claims, unresolved claims, and degree of dependence on management assertions.

## 9. BASE RATES (REFERENCE-CLASS CHECK)

Before any inside-view valuation is accepted at Tier 2 or above, the thesis must answer: of companies in comparable situations historically, what fraction achieved the outcome the thesis requires, and over what horizon?

Examples of required reference classes: companies claiming a durable input chokepoint (what fraction retained pricing power five years on); shortage beneficiaries during the shortage (what fraction of margin gains survived the capacity response); announced turnarounds; premium-multiple compounders (base rate of holding the multiple).

The inside view may exceed the base rate, but the gap must be stated and justified. A thesis that requires an outcome in the top decile of its reference class must say so in those words. This is the cheapest structural defense against optimism the lab possesses.

## 10. INVESTMENT SCORECARD

### 10.1 Pillars and Weights

Seven additive pillars. Failure Risk is deliberately excluded from the sum (see 10.3).

| Pillar | Core question | Weight |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Claim Reality | Is the constraint, compounding engine, or event path genuine and economically material? | 15% |
| Control and Capture | Can the company control the advantage and retain the economics? | 15% |
| Duration | How long can the advantage and scarcity persist? | 10% |
| Financial Translation | Does the advantage create free cash flow and attractive incremental returns? | 15% |
| Mispricing | What specifically does the market misunderstand, and why is it knowable now? | 15% |
| Valuation and Payoff | Is the probability-weighted prospective return attractive at today's price? | 20% |
| Implementability | Can the position be owned efficiently within the portfolio and mandate? | 10% |

### 10.2 Scoring Anchors

Scores without anchors are vibes with decimals. Each pillar is scored 0-10 against written anchors at 2, 5, and 8. Defaults below; refine with use but never delete.

Claim Reality — 2: claim rests mainly on management assertion or thematic narrative. 5: claim supported by industry data but with plausible alternative explanations. 8: claim demonstrated by primary evidence (utilization, lead times, qualified-supplier lists, renewal economics) with alternatives examined and rejected.

Control and Capture — 2: high market share but customers can qualify substitutes within one purchasing cycle. 5: meaningful switching costs or qualification barriers, some customer concentration or contract caps. 8: control mechanism identified and priced (exclusivity, IP, certification, integration depth) with evidence customers have tried and failed to leave.

Duration — 2: capacity response or substitution already announced and funded. 5: advantage likely holds 2-3 years; the erosion path is identifiable. 8: structural barriers to replication credibly exceed 5 years, with the eventual erosion mechanism named anyway.

Financial Translation — 2: advantage visible in revenue but not margins or cash; heavy reinvestment needs with unclear returns. 5: advantage visible in gross margin, partially reaching FCF. 8: advantage flows to FCF with high incremental returns on capital, and management's allocation record supports retention.

Mispricing — 2: "market underappreciates" with no mechanism; the idea is on the cover of the theme. 5: identifiable gap (segment buried in a conglomerate, estimates lagging a disclosed inflection) with partial recognition underway. 8: specific, checkable disagreement with consensus, an identified reason the market is wrong (structural seller, coverage gap, complexity), and a recognition path.

Valuation and Payoff — 2: current price requires top-decile reference-class outcomes to earn a market return. 5: fair value in the base case, attractive only in the bull case. 8: base-case probability-weighted return clearly exceeds hurdle with the bear case near capital preservation.

Implementability — 2: liquidity, borrow, account placement, or tax treatment materially impairs the return. 5: ownable with minor friction. 8: clean fit with account placement, position sizing, and monitoring capacity.

### 10.3 Failure Risk as Gate, Not Weight

Permanent-impairment risk is not an additive score. A 9/10 idea with uninsurable ruin risk is not an 8.1; it is a rejection or a capped position. Failure Risk is assessed as one of three states:

- Pass: bear case is drawdown, not impairment.
- Cap: credible impairment path exists; position capped at Tier 2 sizing regardless of score, with the impairment trigger added to kill lines.
- Veto: impairment probability or severity unacceptable; idea rejected regardless of score.

### 10.4 Submetric Discipline and Judgment Gate

Submetrics may inform a pillar but never receive separate additive weight when they express the same phenomenon (scarcity, pricing power, gross-margin expansion, and incremental margins may all be one constraint; do not count it four times).

The weighted score is evidence, not the decision. No candidate advances automatically on a numeric threshold. Mandatory overrides: unsupported load-bearing claim, unacceptable liquidity, Failure Risk cap or veto, implausible valuation assumptions, excessive portfolio duplication, failed falsification test, unresolved accounting concern.

## 11. VALUE-CAPTURE WATERFALL

Trace the economics through the full chain before selecting a security:

End-Market Expenditure -> Supplier Revenue -> Gross Profit -> Operating Profit -> Free Cash Flow -> Value Accruing to Security Holders

For each step determine: who receives the revenue; who retains gross profit; who funds expansion; who bears working capital; who assumes execution risk; who faces regulatory limits; who captures residual FCF; whether minority shareholders receive that value; whether dilution, debt, taxes, or parent-company structure intercept it.

Expression comparison by tier:

- Tier 2: compare the obvious beneficiary plus the two most plausible alternative expressions, with one sentence each on why remaining categories are excluded.
- Tier 3: compare the full set — direct owner, critical supplier, equipment provider, infrastructure owner, software/data/testing/certification provider, customer with privileged access, parent or holding company, competitor, basket or ETF, and potential short candidate.

The obvious beneficiary cannot be selected until the required comparison is complete.

## 12. DISCOVERY TRACKS, RECOGNITION CLOCK, AND EDGE DECAY

### 12.1 Parallel Discovery Tracks

Run separate discovery tracks so easily measured physical bottlenecks do not crowd out other constraint types: physical capacity; manufacturing and yield; intellectual property; data and workflow; distribution and network; regulatory and certification; trust, security, and authentication; capital, insurance, and risk-bearing; skilled labor and coordination; infrastructure and resources. Each track may use evidence standards appropriate to its constraint type (utilization and lead times for physical; switching behavior, integration depth, error costs, and renewal rates for workflow). Do not force every constraint into a capacity-utilization frame.

### 12.2 Market-Recognition Clock

For every Tier 2+ thesis, identify when observable: T0 earliest evidence the claim exists; T1 first company-level monetization; T2 first material management disclosure; T3 first meaningful sell-side recognition; T4 broad estimate revisions; T5 institutional-ownership acceleration; T6 material rerating; T7 broad thematic consensus. Measure discovery lead time, price movement before recognition, fundamental-confirmation lag, percentage of rerating already realized, remaining valuation gap, and the false-positive rate among ideas identified at comparable stages. "Early" may not be claimed without reference to these milestones.

### 12.3 Edge-Decay Hazards

No precise numerical alpha half-life may be assigned before sufficient historical calibration exists. Classify decay as slow, moderate, fast, event-dependent, or indeterminate. Track hazards separately: information diffusion, valuation adjustment, competitive response, capacity response, regulatory response, and thesis invalidation. A long-lived advantage can coexist with a short-lived mispricing. Numerical decay models are permitted only after the lab has a preserved idea set with discovery dates, stable definitions, and out-of-sample calibration; until then, decay formulas are conceptual aids.

# PART IV — DECISION AND RISK DISCIPLINE

## 13. EXIT AND REVIEW DISCIPLINE

Entry without a pre-registered exit plan is a process violation. Every Tier 2+ position requires, at entry:

1. Kill lines — specific, observable conditions that force exit or freeze regardless of price (pattern: dividend cut, buyback pause, leverage above a stated ceiling, loss of a qualification, adverse reserve development). Kill lines are executed, not renegotiated; a fired kill line may be overridden only by a written Tier 3 re-underwrite completed after flat.
2. Thesis-review cadence — scheduled reviews at each earnings print plus a calendar backstop (no position goes more than one quarter unreviewed). A review that finds the original evidence stale triggers mandatory re-underwrite before any add.
3. Time stop on the mispricing leg — a stated date by which recognition (T3 or better on the clock) should have begun. If it has not, the mispricing thesis is presumed wrong even if the business thesis is intact; the position moves to reduce-or-exit review. Business quality is not a license to wait indefinitely.
4. Add conditions — pre-registered, condition-triggered rather than calendar-scheduled, ANDed with a thesis gate (engine-aware: cash-return names on total-cash-return yield bands, compounders on pullback AND multiple versus own history, cyclicals on P/B or normalized earnings).
5. Staleness rule — any alert, trigger level, or watchlist entry older than two quarters without reconfirmation is presumed stale and may not be acted on until re-underwritten.
6. Calendar Gate (v2.2.1) — every dated decision window carries an explicit close-out obligation: on or before the window's end date, a recorded decision — act, pass, or extend with written reason — must exist. Silent lapse is a process violation of the same severity as entry without an exit plan. A pass is a first-class outcome, recorded with the same care as an entry; the review that produces it may run early. Founding precedent: GMED window closed with explicit PASS on 2026-07-29, two days before expiry, after full Tier 2 underwriting.
7. Window-evidence rule (v2.2.1) — a Tier 1 scan may open a watch item; it may never open a dated decision window. A decision window — any commitment to decide on a name by a date — opens only once Tier 2 evidence exists for that name. A window found to have opened on scan-tier evidence alone is void and closes as pass by default, without prejudice to re-opening properly. Codifies the GMED lesson: the original window was dated off a scan whose central cheapness claim failed Tier 2 verification.

## 14. PROBABILITY CALIBRATION

Calibration is a measurement, not a virtue word. Requirements:

1. Every Tier 2+ thesis states 2-5 explicit probability forecasts with resolution dates ("70 percent that FY27 FCF exceeds X, resolves at FY27 report"; "60 percent that the October trading update reaffirms guidance").
2. Forecasts are logged at entry in the append-only ledger and may not be edited after the fact.
3. On resolution, each forecast is scored (Brier score) and aggregated quarterly into a calibration curve: when the lab says 70 percent, how often does the event occur?
4. Systematic overconfidence in any probability band triggers a written adjustment to how that band is used in expected-return math.
5. Decision quality is scored on process and calibration, independent of outcome. Do not reward a poor process because the stock rose; do not condemn a sound probabilistic decision solely because an adverse outcome occurred.

## 15. DECISION-DISCIPLINE COMMITTEE AND PORTFOLIO CONTROL

### 15.1 Six Disciplines (Tier 3)

Replace persona imitation with six decision disciplines; public investors may be referenced only as illustrations of broad styles.

1. Governance and Forced-Catalyst: misallocated capital, governance failures, separations, balance-sheet actions, realistic catalysts. May not assume activism is feasible.
2. Statistical Signal and Falsification: robustness, data quality, out-of-sample behavior, regime dependence, multiple testing, economic rationale. May not approve a signal merely because a backtest is attractive.
3. Multi-Strategy and Relative Value: alternative expressions, pairs, baskets, event paths, liquidity, capital efficiency, hedging. Must identify the simplest efficient expression.
4. Concentrated Quality: pricing power, reinvestment, allocation, cash-flow durability, impairment risk, suitability for concentration. May not treat quality as valuation-independent.
5. Reflexive Macro and Capital Flow: policy response, financing conditions, capital formation, narrative-price feedback, boom-bust behavior. Must specify the feedback loop rather than invoke "reflexivity."
6. Factor, Valuation, and Crowding: value, quality, momentum, carry, factor contamination, ownership, consensus, crowding. Must distinguish genuine company-specific insight from repackaged factor exposure.

Each discipline discloses: conclusion, confidence, evidence used, assumptions, what it is likely to miss, and conditions under which another discipline should receive greater weight. At Tier 2, one adversarial review drawn from the most relevant discipline substitutes for the full committee.

### 15.2 Portfolio-Level Exposure Control

Before any Tier 2+ entry: identify the position's dominant economic drivers and factor exposures; check aggregate exposure to each shared driver across all sleeves and accounts (including ETF look-through); apply named factor caps where concentration risk exists (pattern: the tobacco factor cap); reject false diversification — several individually attractive names on one driver are one position for risk purposes and are sized as such, or the best single expression is chosen.

# PART V — VALIDATION

## 16. COMPOSITE SYNTHETIC GATES

Gate 0 (document lint, non-blocking): confirm the prompt and process contain each required mechanism (tiering, universe filter, architectural independence, genealogy, anchored scorecard, waterfall, recognition clock, hazard decay, calibration ledger, exit discipline, baselines, simplicity audit, point-in-time protocol, portfolio control). This checks presence of text, not behavior; it never substitutes for Gates A-F.

Each behavioral gate uses controlled input with a known failure mode, an expected correct decision defined before the run, the actual decision, evidence used, pass/fail, remediation, rerun result, and sign-off. A failed gate stops that gate's dependents; fix and rerun before relying on the tested capability. Gates may be run in any order.

Gate A — Claim Authenticity (absorbs old Gates 1, 4, 5). Three variants: (i) high-share company whose customers can qualify substitutes quickly — reject structural classification; (ii) cyclical shortage from outages or inventory rebuild — normalize margins, model announced capacity, classify cyclical absent contrary evidence; (iii) management-generated illusion with deteriorating FCF and customers qualifying alternatives — downgrade management assertions, elevate customer and supplier evidence.

Gate B — Rent Capture and Expression (absorbs 2, 8). Two variants: (i) indispensable business with regulated, capped, or competed-away returns — recognize systemic importance, reject shareholder value capture; (ii) obvious beneficiary is capital-intensive and expensive while a supplier controls the scarcer capability — complete the waterfall and select the superior expression.

Gate C — Valuation Discipline (absorbs 3, 7). Two variants: (i) exceptional business at a price requiring implausible assumptions — preserve the quality rating, reject purchase, set re-entry conditions; (ii) real bottleneck already fully discounted by valuation and ownership — affirm the business thesis, reject or reduce the alpha thesis, move to watchlist.

Gate D — Contamination (absorbs 9, 10). Two variants: (i) identical economics presented once with a fashionable label and once anonymized — pre-reveal scores must be materially similar, unexplained deltas investigated; (ii) numerous articles derived from one promotional source — collapse to one genealogy, downgrade corroboration, flag unsupported load-bearing claims.

Gate E — Statistical and Regime Integrity (absorbs 6, 11). Two variants: (i) backtest contaminated by survivorship, tuning, look-ahead, and zero costs — reject and identify each contamination; (ii) thesis stress under recession, higher rates, lower commodity prices, currency moves, customer consolidation, regulatory intervention, rapid capacity expansion, and substitution — distinguish reduced upside from thesis destruction, produce explicit kill conditions.

Gate F — Portfolio and Process (absorbs 12, 13). Two variants: (i) several individually attractive securities on one economic driver — identify shared exposure, reject false diversification, cap or consolidate; (ii) full lab and a simple checklist reach the same conclusion — record that complexity added no value and flag stages for removal.

## 17. BASELINES AND THE SIMPLICITY AUDITOR

Two mandatory baselines per research cycle:

1. One mechanical screen appropriate to the engine (quality, valuation, or momentum/revision).
2. The named human baseline: Jim's own competent three-hour analysis of the same candidate, done before seeing the lab's full output.

The remaining baselines (market-cap benchmark, equal-weight universe, the other screens, consensus view) rotate quarterly as calibration exercises rather than running on every idea.

The full system must demonstrate incremental decision value against the mandatory baselines: rejecting a false positive, finding a hidden beneficiary, improving valuation accuracy or calibration, identifying a decisive risk, selecting a better expression, catching duplication, or discovering the idea materially earlier. If it does not, use the simpler method.

The Simplicity Auditor is permanent, holds veto authority over unnecessary process, and reports for every finalist: what changed because of the full process, whether the change was useful, whether it justified the cost, which stages were redundant, and what should be removed, merged, or automated. A more elaborate report is not evidence of greater analytical value.

## 18. POINT-IN-TIME HISTORICAL VALIDATION

Synthetic gates validate decision logic; they do not establish investment skill. Historical testing uses only information available on the assigned cutoff date. The frozen packet excludes later filings, later restatements (unless separately identified), knowledge of future winners or bankruptcies, later index composition, later estimates, later industry outcomes, and retrospective descriptions of inevitability.

Protocol per case: establish the cutoff date; freeze the evidence set; record the system's thesis and probability estimates; record the baseline conclusions; reveal subsequent information in chronological increments; score calibration, timing, valuation, and falsification; never revise the original forecast after the outcome is known.

The initial case set is drawn from the lab's own preserved research records (Appendix A), because those have real discovery dates and real decision documents — the two things third-party reconstructions cannot supply. Third-party historical cases (failed technologies, accounting frauds, regulatory reversals, famous chokepoint winners) are added later as a second wave once the protocol is proven on internal cases.

Masking principle — evidence in, verdicts out (v2.2.1): run records and frozen packets keep evidence and verdicts structurally separable. A blind-rerun context receives the evidence set only; it may never receive prior verdicts, scores, derived levels, resolution notes, or forward observations. Any context that has seen a verdict for a case is permanently contaminated for blind-rerun purposes on that case and must be recorded as such — reruns therefore execute in separated agent calls (AlphaGPT or equivalent) fed evidence-only packets, never in the working conversation that produced or discussed the verdict. Contamination is a property of the context, not the operator; noting it is bookkeeping, not failure.

## 19. FORWARD PAPER-PORTFOLIO VALIDATION AND THE PROCESS-METRICS PRINCIPLE

Before each paper selection, record: thesis, discovery date, recognition stage, valuation, expected-return distribution, position size, benchmark, falsification conditions, and monitoring schedule. Track absolute, benchmark-relative, and factor-adjusted return; drawdown; thesis and catalyst accuracy; estimate revision; time to recognition; decay classification; costs; turnover; concentration; and decision quality independent of outcome. The benchmark may not be changed after observing performance.

Process-Metrics Principle (named and binding): at realistic idea flow, forward returns will not be statistically distinguishable from luck for several years. The near-term validation currency is therefore process metrics — calibration curves, thesis-accuracy rates, gate false-positive rejection, pre/post-reveal deltas, and Simplicity Auditor findings. Returns are the long-term currency. The lab may not grade itself on paper P&L in year one, in either direction.

## 20. PARALLEL EXECUTION TRACKS

The v2.1 sequential launch order is replaced. Three tracks run concurrently:

Track 1 — Gates: run the document lint, then Gates A-F. A failure blocks reliance on the tested capability, not the whole program.
Track 2 — Historical: build the point-in-time packets for the Appendix A cases and run them under Section 18.
Track 3 — Live paper: the lab may generate and log live ideas immediately, paper-only, under Tier rules. No real capital moves on a lab recommendation until Gates A-F pass and at least four historical cases are complete. Real capital additionally requires the Section 21 sign-offs.

All three tracks feed one permanent, append-only audit trail.
